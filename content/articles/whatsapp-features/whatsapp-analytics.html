WhatsApp analytics shows what happens to every message you send and every chat you receive. It covers delivery, reads, replies, clicks and conversions for campaigns, plus response and resolution times for your team. With the right reports, you can see which messages make money, which annoy customers and where your team needs help. Without them, WhatsApp is guesswork.

Why WhatsApp needs its own reports

Website and ad analytics do not see inside WhatsApp. Once a customer taps "chat", the journey moves to a place your web tools cannot track. So you need reports from the WhatsApp platform itself, linked back to your CRM or store. That way, you can follow a customer from the first message to the final sale.

Message metrics

  • Sent and delivered: the gap shows wrong numbers and people not on WhatsApp.
  • Read: how many opened the message. Some users turn off read receipts, so this is a minimum.
  • Replied: the clearest sign of interest.
  • Button clicks: taps on quick replies and links.
  • Blocks and opt-outs: the early warning that a message missed the mark.

Read WhatsApp analytics per template and per segment, not just in total. In fact, a campaign average can hide one segment that loves the message and another that blocks it.

Team and support metrics

  • First response time: how quickly a person replies after the bot hands off.
  • Resolution time: how long it takes to close a chat.
  • Bot containment: the share of chats the bot resolves alone.
  • Chats per agent: workload and coverage gaps.
  • Customer rating: a one-tap score sent after the chat closes.

These come from the team inbox and the AI bot.

Revenue and cost metrics

The numbers that matter most to owners are money in and money out. To track them:

  1. Add tracked links or coupon codes to templates.
  2. Link orders and deals back to the chat in your CRM or store.
  3. Add the message cost for each send, using Meta's rates.
  4. Divide cost by results, for example cost per booking or per order.

Then you can compare WhatsApp with email, SMS or ads on equal terms. CRM integration makes this link much easier.

Account health

Meta shows a quality rating and messaging limit for each number in WhatsApp Manager. Check them after every large send. If quality drops, slow down and review the templates that caused blocks. Our WhatsApp Business Manager walkthrough shows where to find these.

Building a simple weekly WhatsApp analytics report

A one-page report is enough for most teams:

  • Campaigns sent this week, with delivered, read, replied and converted.
  • Top and bottom template by reply rate.
  • Response and resolution times by team.
  • Revenue and cost from WhatsApp.
  • Quality rating and any warnings.

Next, pick one thing to improve each week. For example, rewrite the worst template, or add cover to the slowest shift. Small, steady changes add up.

Reading benchmarks carefully

You will see many claims about WhatsApp open rates. Treat them with care. Results vary widely by industry, audience and message type. Instead, compare each campaign with your own past results. Our WhatsApp statistics page lists the figures that have clear sources.
