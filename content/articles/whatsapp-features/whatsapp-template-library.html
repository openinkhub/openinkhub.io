A WhatsApp template library is your set of approved message templates, organised so your team can find and reuse them quickly. On the WhatsApp Business Platform, any message you start outside the 24-hour service window must use a template that Meta has approved. So templates are the backbone of reminders, updates and campaigns. A tidy library saves time, avoids rejections and keeps your brand voice consistent.

Why WhatsApp templates exist

Templates protect users from spam. Before a business can message someone first, Meta checks the wording and category. Once approved, the template can be sent many times with different details filled in, such as a name or order number. In short, templates let businesses message at scale while users keep control.

The three template categories

  • Marketing: offers, launches, reminders to buy, newsletters and win-back messages.
  • Utility: updates about something the customer already did, such as an order, booking, payment or account change.
  • Authentication: one-time passcodes for login or verification.

The category affects both approval and price. If a template mixes an order update with an offer, Meta treats it as marketing. Therefore, keep utility templates purely informational. Our guide to per-message pricing shows how categories affect cost.

Parts of a WhatsApp template

  1. Header (optional): text, image, video or document.
  2. Body: the main message, with variables such as {{1}} for the name.
  3. Footer (optional): a short line, for example "Reply STOP to opt out".
  4. Buttons (optional): quick replies, a call button, a website link or a copy-code button.

Meta's template documentation lists the current limits for each part.

Getting templates approved

Most rejections in a WhatsApp template library are avoidable. Follow these rules:

  • Choose the correct category.
  • Give every variable clear text around it, so the reviewer understands it.
  • Add realistic sample values when you submit.
  • Avoid vague or threatening language, and do not ask for sensitive data such as card numbers.
  • Check that the product or service is allowed under WhatsApp's commerce policy.

Many templates are reviewed within minutes. If one is rejected, edit it and resubmit, rather than sending many near-copies.

Organising your WhatsApp template library

As your library grows, naming matters. Use a clear pattern such as category_purpose_language_version, for example utility_order_shipped_hi_v2. Then group templates by journey: onboarding, orders, appointments, payments, campaigns and feedback. Also retire old versions so agents do not send outdated offers. Finally, keep a short note with each template explaining when to use it.

Languages and versions

If you serve customers in several languages, submit each template in each language under the same name. WhatsApp then sends the right version based on the language you choose for the customer. Test each translation with a native speaker, because a stiff translation lowers replies.
