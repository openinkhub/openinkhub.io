WhatsApp broadcast messaging on the official API lets you send one approved message to thousands of opted-in customers at once. Each person receives it as a private one-to-one chat, and their replies come back to your inbox. Unlike unofficial bulk senders, the API route keeps your number safe, shows your verified business name and gives you delivery and read reports for every message.

Broadcast lists vs API broadcasts

The WhatsApp Business App has broadcast lists. They are free, but each list is small, and only people who saved your number receive the message. That works for a small shop. However, it breaks down at scale. API broadcasts have no saved-contact rule and can reach your whole opted-in audience. They also let you personalise each message with the customer's name, order or city. For a step-by-step comparison, see how to send a WhatsApp broadcast message.

What you need before sending

  • Opt-in. Every contact must have agreed to receive messages from you on WhatsApp.
  • An approved template. Broadcasts are business-initiated, so they must use a template Meta has approved.
  • A clean list. Remove duplicates, wrong numbers and people who opted out.
  • The right category. Offers are marketing. Order and account updates are utility.

Skipping opt-in is the fastest way to lose your number. So we always start a broadcast project by checking where each contact came from.

Segmenting your audience

In WhatsApp broadcast messaging, sending the same message to everyone wastes money and invites blocks. Instead, split your list by what you know. For example, segment by past purchases, city, language, last active date or lead stage. Then write a version for each segment. A smaller, relevant send usually earns more replies than a large generic one.

Limits and quality rating

Each number has a daily limit on how many customers it can message first. New numbers start low, and the limit grows as you send messages people welcome. Meanwhile, Meta tracks a quality rating based on blocks and reports. If quality drops, templates can be paused. Therefore, the safest plan is to grow volume step by step and watch the rating after each send. Our guide to bulk WhatsApp messages covers this in more depth.

Personalising broadcasts

Templates can carry variables such as the customer's name, order number or appointment time. They can also include an image, video or document header, plus buttons for quick replies, calls or links. For instance, a festive offer can show the product image, use the customer's name and add a "Shop now" button. Our template library guide explains each part.

Reading delivery reports

After each broadcast, check four numbers:

  1. Sent and delivered: failures often mean wrong numbers or people not on WhatsApp.
  2. Read: shows how many opened the message.
  3. Replied or clicked: shows real interest.
  4. Blocked or opted out: an early warning sign.

Then compare segments and templates, and repeat what worked. WhatsApp analytics describes the full set of reports.
