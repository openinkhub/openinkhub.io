Technical SEO is the behind-the-scenes work that helps search engines find, crawl, render and index your website. It covers HTTPS, XML sitemaps, robots.txt, canonical tags, redirects, structured data, page speed, mobile-friendliness and clean internal linking. Your website needs it because even excellent content cannot rank if Google cannot reach it, understand it or load it quickly. Think of it as the foundation that content and links are built on.

What technical SEO covers

AreaWhat to check
Crawlingrobots.txt, internal links, crawl errors, orphan pages
Indexingnoindex tags, canonical URLs, duplicate content, sitemaps
RenderingContent visible in HTML, JavaScript issues
SpeedCore Web Vitals, server response, images
MobileResponsive layout, tap targets, readable text
SecurityHTTPS everywhere, no mixed content
Structured dataSchema for articles, products, FAQs, organisation
Redirects301s for moved pages, no chains or loops

Why your website needs it

  • Visibility: pages that are not indexed cannot rank at all.
  • Efficiency: search engines spend their crawl time on pages that matter.
  • Rich results: structured data can earn stars, FAQs and product details in results.
  • Experience: fast, mobile-friendly pages keep visitors and convert better.
  • Protection: correct redirects keep rankings during redesigns and migrations.

A technical SEO audit checklist

  1. Check Search Console for indexing errors and manual actions.
  2. Crawl the site to find broken links, redirect chains and duplicate titles.
  3. Review robots.txt and the XML sitemap.
  4. Confirm canonical tags point to the right versions.
  5. Test key templates with the URL Inspection tool to see rendered HTML.
  6. Measure Core Web Vitals on real-user data.
  7. Validate structured data with the Rich Results Test.
  8. Check HTTPS, mixed content and security headers.
  9. Review internal linking and click depth.
  10. Check hreflang if you target several languages or countries.

Google's crawling and indexing documentation explains each topic in detail.

What it costs

A one-time audit for a small business website is usually affordable, and many fixes are quick. Large sites, ecommerce stores with thousands of URLs and migrations need more time. Ongoing monitoring is often part of a monthly SEO or maintenance plan. In India, technical work is frequently bundled with on-page SEO and content.

Platform notes

WordPress handles many basics well with a good SEO plugin, though plugins can also create duplicate archive pages. Shopify fixes some URL patterns you cannot change. Custom and JavaScript sites need extra care with rendering; see JavaScript SEO.

Common technical SEO mistakes

First, leaving a "noindex" tag from staging on the live site. Next, blocking CSS or JavaScript files in robots.txt. Then, filter and sort parameters that create thousands of near-duplicate pages. After that, redirect chains after several redesigns. Finally, ignoring Search Console warnings for months.

How often to review it

In addition to a full audit once or twice a year, check Search Console every month. Also review technical SEO after any redesign, platform change or large content launch, because these are the moments when errors usually appear. As a result, small issues are fixed before they cost traffic.
