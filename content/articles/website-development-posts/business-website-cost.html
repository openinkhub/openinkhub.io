A business website cost in India usually falls between ₹30,000 and ₹3 lakh. At the lower end, you get a clean, template-based site with a few pages. In the middle, you get custom design, a content system, SEO setup and lead forms. At the top, you get a larger site with integrations, multiple languages or advanced features. Beyond the build, plan for hosting, maintenance and content updates every year.

What you get at each budget

₹15,000 – ₹50,000: starter site

Five to eight pages on a ready-made theme, a contact form, basic on-page SEO and mobile-friendly layout. Good for new businesses, consultants and local shops that need a credible online presence quickly.

₹50,000 – ₹1.5 lakh: growth site

Custom or heavily adapted design, a CMS your team can edit, service pages built for search, a blog, analytics, speed work and integrations such as WhatsApp or a CRM. This is the sweet spot for most small and medium businesses.

₹1.5 lakh – ₹3 lakh and above: advanced site

Larger sites with many templates, multiple locations or languages, booking or quoting tools, member areas, deeper SEO and stronger performance. Custom web applications sit above this range.

Costs people forget

  • Content: copywriting, professional photos and video.
  • Premium tools: paid themes, plugins and stock images.
  • Business email: usually billed per user per month.
  • Renewals: domain, hosting and licences every year.
  • Changes after launch: new pages and features later on.
  • GST: check whether quotes include it.

Asking about these upfront avoids surprises.

Is a cheap website a false saving?

Sometimes. A very cheap site may be slow, poorly built for search or hard to update. Then you pay again to fix it. However, a simple, well-built site can work very well for a small business. The question is not the price but the result: does the site load fast, rank for the right searches and turn visitors into enquiries? How to get value for your money

  1. Write down the goal of the site, such as leads, bookings or credibility.
  2. List the must-have pages and features.
  3. Prepare your content before the project starts.
  4. Ask for a fixed scope with clear deliverables.
  5. Make sure you own the domain, hosting and code.
  6. Check what support is included after launch.

Payment terms

Most agencies ask for an advance, often 30% to 50%, and split the rest across milestones such as design approval and launch. Avoid paying the full amount before seeing the design. Also confirm in writing what happens if the scope changes.

Business website cost vs return

Think of the site as a sales tool. If a ₹1 lakh website brings two extra clients a month, it pays for itself quickly. On the other hand, a site no one finds earns nothing at any price. So budget for SEO and content too, not just design. Google's SEO starter guide explains the basics.
