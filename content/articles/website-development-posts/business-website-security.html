Business website security comes down to a handful of habits done consistently: run everything over HTTPS, keep the CMS, themes and plugins updated, protect logins with strong passwords and two-factor authentication, put a web application firewall in front of the site, take regular off-site backups, scan for malware and monitor for problems. Add a simple plan for what to do if something goes wrong. Most attacks on small business websites are automated and target known weaknesses, so the basics stop most of them.

A business website security checklist

  1. Force HTTPS across the whole site.
  2. Update the CMS, themes and plugins promptly, and remove unused ones.
  3. Use unique, strong passwords and two-factor login for every admin.
  4. Give each person only the access they need, and remove old accounts.
  5. Put a web application firewall and CDN in front of the site.
  6. Take automatic daily backups stored off the server, and test restores.
  7. Scan for malware and monitor file changes.
  8. Set up uptime monitoring and login alerts.
  9. Keep hosting, domain and DNS accounts locked with two-factor login.
  10. Write a one-page incident plan: who to call and what to do.

What website security costs

Good hosting usually includes SSL, basic firewalls and backups. Security plugins have free and paid tiers. A managed firewall and CDN, professional monitoring and malware clean-up cover add monthly costs, often bundled into a maintenance plan. A security audit or penetration test for a custom app is a one-time cost. Our website maintenance cost guide shows typical plans.

Monitoring for threats

  • Uptime alerts by email or WhatsApp.
  • Malware and blocklist scans.
  • Alerts for new admin users or failed logins.
  • File integrity checks for unexpected changes.
  • Search Console's security issues report.

Platform notes

WordPress is safe when kept updated and lean; most hacks come from outdated plugins. Shopify handles platform security, but you still control staff accounts and apps. Custom sites need secure coding and regular dependency updates, guided by the OWASP Top Ten.

Common website security mistakes

First, sharing one admin login among the whole team. Next, keeping backups only on the same server. Then, ignoring update notices for months. After that, installing nulled or pirated themes. Finally, assuming a small business is "too small to target".

Security and SEO

A hacked site can be flagged in search results or drop in rankings because of spam pages. Strong security protects your traffic as well as your data.
