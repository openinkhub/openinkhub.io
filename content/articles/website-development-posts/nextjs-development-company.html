A Next.js development company plans, builds and maintains websites and web apps with Next.js, the React framework for server rendering, static generation and routing. To choose the right one, look past the portfolio: check their live sites' Core Web Vitals, ask how they decide between static and server rendering, review how they integrate a headless CMS, and confirm their testing, deployment and support process. Questions to ask a Next.js development company

  1. Can you show three live Next.js sites, and what are their PageSpeed results?
  2. How do you decide which pages are static and which are dynamic?
  3. Which headless CMS do you recommend for us, and why?
  4. How do you handle redirects, metadata and sitemaps for SEO?
  5. Where will the site be hosted, and what will hosting cost?
  6. Who owns the repository, and what does support include?

Testing their claims is easy: run their portfolio through PageSpeed Insights yourself.

Pricing models

  • Fixed scope: marketing sites and content-led builds.
  • Phased: discovery first, then build in milestones.
  • Dedicated team: SaaS products and evolving apps.

Remember running costs too: hosting, CMS plans and monitoring. Our Vercel vs traditional hosting guide covers hosting choices.

Typical timelines

A marketing site with a headless CMS usually takes four to ten weeks. Apps with accounts, payments or complex integrations take several months. A partner should give you a staging preview for every milestone so you can review progress.

Red flags

First, every page rendered on the server with no caching, which raises costs and slows pages. Next, no plan for content editing. Then, client-only pages that hurt SEO. Finally, no handover documentation.

Is Next.js right for you?

Next.js suits fast marketing sites, content-heavy sites, headless stores and SaaS front ends. If your team mainly edits pages and blogs and needs plugins, WordPress may still be simpler. Our Next.js website development guide explains the platform itself.

Next.js vs React: what you are paying for

Some clients ask whether they need Next.js development services or plain React. In short, Next.js adds the parts a public website needs: routing, server rendering, static pages, image optimisation and metadata handling. As a result, pages arrive as complete HTML and tend to score well on Core Web Vitals, which helps Next.js SEO. Plain React suits apps behind a login where search visibility does not matter. Therefore, if you hire Next.js developers, ask them to explain which parts of your site truly need dynamic rendering and which can be static and cheaper to host.
