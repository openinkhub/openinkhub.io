Website maintenance cost in India usually ranges from ₹1,500 to ₹25,000 a month. A small brochure site with occasional updates sits at the low end. A busy ecommerce store or custom web app with security monitoring, regular changes and fast support sits at the high end. Another way to budget is 15% to 20% of the original build cost each year. Skipping maintenance often costs more later, when something breaks or gets hacked.

What website maintenance includes

  • Software updates: CMS core, theme and plugins.
  • Backups: regular, stored off the server, and tested.
  • Security: malware scans, firewall and login protection.
  • Uptime monitoring: alerts if the site goes down.
  • Performance: speed checks and image optimisation.
  • Content changes: small text, image and page updates.
  • Fixes: broken links, forms and layout issues.
  • Reports: a monthly summary of work done.

A good provider explains how often each task runs and sends proof, such as backup logs.

Yearly costs outside the plan

Some costs are billed separately. These include domain renewal, hosting, premium plugin and theme licences, SSL certificates if not free, and business email. Ask whether your plan includes hosting, because some do and some do not. What makes maintenance cost more

  1. Ecommerce stores, because orders and payments must never break.
  2. Many plugins or extensions, which need frequent updates.
  3. Custom code, which only skilled developers can safely change.
  4. High traffic or uptime needs.
  5. Fast response times, such as same-day fixes.

DIY maintenance vs a plan

A small site owner can handle basic updates and backups with a little training. However, updates sometimes break things, and fixing them needs technical skill. Also, security problems are easy to miss. For business-critical sites, a plan is usually cheaper than an emergency repair. For example, cleaning a hacked site and restoring rankings can cost far more than a year of maintenance.

How to choose a maintenance plan

First, check what is included and what counts as extra work. Next, ask how often backups run and where they are stored. Then confirm response times for urgent problems. Finally, make sure you keep full access to your site, hosting and domain. The WordPress team's hardening guide is a good reference for what good security looks like, and our website maintenance checklist lists the tasks a good plan should cover.

Signs your maintenance is not working

Watch for warning signs. For instance, plugins that show updates waiting for months, backups nobody has ever restored, forms that quietly stop sending emails, or a site that gets slower every quarter. Also check whether you receive a report each month. If you notice any of these, your current plan needs a review, because small problems tend to grow into expensive ones.
