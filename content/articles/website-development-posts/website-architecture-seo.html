Website architecture is how your pages are organised and linked together. A clear structure, with a logical hierarchy, readable URLs, hub pages that lead to related subpages and descriptive internal links, helps search engines find every page, understand which pages matter most and see how topics connect. It also helps visitors find what they need. Good architecture is one of the most underrated SEO advantages, because it multiplies the value of every page you publish.

How website architecture affects SEO

  • Discovery: pages linked from other pages get crawled; orphan pages often do not.
  • Priority: pages close to the home page and linked often are seen as more important.
  • Topical authority: grouped, interlinked content signals depth on a subject.
  • Relevance: anchor text tells search engines what the linked page is about.
  • User experience: clear navigation keeps visitors moving and converting.

Flat, deep and clustered structures

StructureDescriptionVerdict
DeepImportant pages buried five or more clicks downAvoid
FlatEverything linked from the home pageMessy at scale
Hub and spokeHub pages link to subpages, which link back and to each otherBest for most sites

For example, a service hub such as "website development" links to subpages on cost, process and platforms, and each of those links back to the hub and to closely related articles.

Structuring internal links

  1. Keep important pages within three clicks of the home page.
  2. Give each topic a hub page that links to all its subpages.
  3. Link from subpages back to the hub and to related siblings.
  4. Add contextual links inside the body, not only in menus and footers.
  5. Link from strong, popular pages to new pages you want to rank.
  6. Use breadcrumbs to show hierarchy.

Choosing anchor text

Use descriptive anchor text that tells readers what they will get, such as "website maintenance checklist" rather than "click here". Vary it naturally, and avoid stuffing the exact same keyword into every link. Google's guidance on crawlable links and anchor text covers the details.

URL structure

Keep URLs short, readable and stable. Use hyphens, lowercase words and no unnecessary parameters. Most importantly, once a URL ranks, do not change it without a 301 redirect, because changing URLs throws away earned signals.

Auditing your architecture

  • Crawl the site to find orphan pages, broken links and redirect chains.
  • Check click depth for key pages.
  • Review Search Console's links report for the most-linked pages.
  • Look for pages competing for the same keyword and consolidate them.
  • Check that navigation works on mobile too.

Common architecture mistakes

First, menus that link to everything, which dilutes focus. Next, blog posts that never link to service pages. Then, tag and archive pages in a CMS such as WordPress creating thin duplicates. After that, changing URLs during a redesign without redirects. Finally, JavaScript menus that search engines cannot follow; see JavaScript SEO.
