AI in website development is changing both how websites are built and what they can do. On the building side, AI helps teams draft designs, write and review code, create content and test faster. On the visitor side, AI adds chatbots that answer questions, smart search that understands intent, personalised recommendations and automated support. Used well, it speeds up delivery and improves user experience. Used carelessly, it brings inaccurate answers, privacy risks and generic content.

How AI is changing the build process

  • Design: AI tools generate layout ideas, images and variations to explore quickly.
  • Code: AI assistants suggest code, explain errors and write tests, which speeds up developers.
  • Content: AI drafts outlines and first versions that writers then edit and fact-check.
  • Testing: AI helps generate test cases and spot visual or accessibility issues.
  • SEO: AI helps research topics, cluster keywords and suggest internal links.

However, AI output still needs expert review. Generated code can contain bugs or security holes, and generated content can be wrong or bland.

What AI features cost

Costs vary. A ready-made chatbot connected to your content may cost a modest monthly fee plus setup. Custom features that use your own data, such as a search engine over thousands of products, cost more to build, and AI usage is billed by volume. So estimate expected traffic before choosing. Risks to manage

  • Accuracy: AI can answer confidently and wrongly; ground it in your own content and review answers.
  • Privacy: do not send personal data to AI services without consent and clear policies.
  • Bias: test outputs across different user groups.
  • Generic content: AI-written pages without real expertise rarely rank or convert.
  • Security: review AI-written code like any other code.

Google's guidance on using generative AI content explains how it treats AI-assisted pages.

Common AI website mistakes

First, publishing AI content without editing, which harms trust and SEO. Next, adding a chatbot that cannot hand off to a human. Then, ignoring speed: heavy AI widgets can hurt Core Web Vitals. Finally, adding AI because it is trendy rather than to solve a real problem.

Getting started

Start with one clear problem, such as slow support replies or poor site search, and measure the result.
