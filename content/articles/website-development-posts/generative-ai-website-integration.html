Generative AI integration can make a website more useful for visitors and cheaper to run for your team. On the visitor side, it powers support chat, natural-language search, summaries of long pages and instant translation. On the team side, it drafts product descriptions, writes first versions of content and helps fill forms. The key is to use it for specific jobs, ground it in your own content and keep people reviewing what matters.

Practical use cases

For example, an electronics store with 3,000 products can draft unique descriptions from technical specs, then have staff edit the top sellers. A law firm can offer a plain-language summary at the top of long articles. A college can answer admission questions from its prospectus, around the clock. Each of these solves a real problem that visitors or staff already have.

How generative AI integration works

  1. Pick the job and the success measure, such as fewer support emails.
  2. Choose an AI model and provider, considering cost, quality and data policies.
  3. Connect your content, often through retrieval, so answers come from your own pages. See our RAG assistant guide.
  4. Build the feature into your website or CMS, with limits and logging.
  5. Test with real examples, including edge cases.
  6. Launch, monitor quality and costs, and improve.

What it costs

There are two parts: setup and usage. Setup covers design, development and testing. Usage is billed by the AI provider based on how much text is processed. A small feature on a modest site can be affordable, while search or chat over large document sets costs more. Therefore, set usage limits and monitor spending from day one. Risks to manage

  • Wrong answers: ground the model in your content and show sources.
  • Privacy: do not send personal data without consent and a clear purpose.
  • Copyright: check rights for any generated images or text.
  • Bland content: AI drafts need expert editing to be useful.
  • Cost spikes: cap usage and watch for abuse.

Generative AI and SEO

Google does not ban AI-assisted content, but it rewards helpful, original pages written for people. Mass-producing thin pages to game rankings can backfire. Read Google's guidance on using generative AI content. In short, use AI to speed up drafting, and use experts to add the insight, accuracy and experience that make content worth ranking.

Common generative AI mistakes

First, launching a chatbot that answers from general knowledge rather than your content. Next, publishing AI text without review. Then, adding heavy scripts that slow the page and hurt Core Web Vitals. Finally, chasing features without a clear business goal.
