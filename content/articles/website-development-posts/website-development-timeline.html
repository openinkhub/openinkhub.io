A website development timeline depends on the type of site. A simple brochure site takes about two to four weeks. A business website with custom design, a CMS and SEO usually takes six to twelve weeks. An ecommerce store or custom web application often takes three to six months. Most delays come from late content, slow feedback and scope changes, not from the coding itself.

Timeline for each stage

For a typical eight-to-ten-week business website, the stages often look like this:

  1. Discovery and planning (1–2 weeks): goals, audience, sitemap and scope.
  2. Wireframes and UX (1 week): page layouts and user journeys.
  3. Visual design (1–2 weeks): designs for key pages, with a round of changes.
  4. Development (2–4 weeks): building pages, CMS and integrations.
  5. Content and SEO (in parallel): writing, loading content, metadata and schema.
  6. Testing (1 week): devices, browsers, forms, speed and fixes.
  7. Launch (a few days): going live, redirects and monitoring.

What causes delays

  • Late content. Text and images are the most common bottleneck.
  • Slow feedback. A week waiting for approval adds a week to launch.
  • Too many decision makers. Conflicting opinions cause rework.
  • Scope creep. New features added mid-project push dates back.
  • Missing access. Hosting, domain or third-party logins not shared on time.
  • Integration surprises. Third-party systems that behave unexpectedly.

How to launch on schedule

First, agree the scope in writing before design starts. Next, name one person who gives final approval. Then prepare content early, ideally before development begins. Also set feedback deadlines, for example two working days per review. Finally, keep a list of "phase two" ideas, so new requests do not derail the current launch.

Can a website be built faster?

Yes, with trade-offs. A template site with ready content can launch within a week or two. However, custom design, careful SEO and proper testing take time. Rushing usually means fixing problems after launch. A better approach is to launch a focused first version quickly, then add pages and features in phases.

Timelines and cost

Longer projects usually cost more, because they involve more work. On the other hand, a very tight deadline can also raise the price, since the team must prioritise your project. Our website development cost guide covers pricing by project type.

What happens after launch

Launch is not the end. Plan for a few weeks of monitoring, small fixes and early improvements based on real visitors. Google's Search Console guide explains how to watch indexing after launch.
