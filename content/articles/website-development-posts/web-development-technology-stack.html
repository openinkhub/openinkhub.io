A web development technology stack is the set of tools a website runs on: the front end visitors see, the backend logic, the database, the CMS, hosting and supporting services. To choose the right technology stack, start from the project's needs, budget, timeline and who will maintain it, then pick proven, widely used tools that your team or partner knows well. The stack strongly affects cost: WordPress or Shopify builds are usually cheapest, while custom stacks cost more but offer more flexibility.

The layers of a technology stack

LayerCommon options
Front endHTML/CSS, React, Next.js, Vue, Angular
BackendNode.js, PHP with Laravel, Python with Django or FastAPI
DatabaseMySQL, PostgreSQL, MongoDB
CMSWordPress, headless CMS, Shopify for stores
HostingShared, VPS, cloud, Vercel or other platforms
ServicesPayments, email, search, analytics, monitoring

Sample stacks by project

  • Business website: WordPress on managed hosting with a CDN.
  • Online store: Shopify, or WooCommerce on WordPress.
  • Fast marketing site: Next.js or Astro with a headless CMS.
  • Customer portal: Laravel or Django with MySQL or PostgreSQL.
  • SaaS product: Next.js front end, Node.js or NestJS backend, PostgreSQL.
  • AI-heavy app: Python with FastAPI, plus a vector database.

How to choose the right technology stack

  1. Define requirements: features, users, content, integrations and expected growth.
  2. Consider who maintains it: in-house skills, your agency and the hiring market.
  3. Prefer proven tools with large communities and long-term support.
  4. Match budget and timeline: ready platforms launch faster.
  5. Check hosting and running costs over three years, not just the build.
  6. Think about SEO and performance, especially rendering and speed.
  7. Keep it simple: fewer moving parts mean fewer things to break.

How the stack affects cost

A WordPress business site costs far less than a custom React and Node.js platform, both to build and to maintain. However, forcing a complex product into a basic platform can cost more later in workarounds. In addition, rare technologies raise hiring costs. Therefore, choose the simplest stack that meets real requirements for the next few years.

Future-proofing

  • Choose widely used, actively maintained tools.
  • Own your code, data, domain and hosting accounts.
  • Use standard APIs so parts can be replaced later.
  • Plan regular upgrades of frameworks and dependencies.
  • Document the architecture so new developers can join quickly.

Surveys such as the Stack Overflow Developer Survey show which technologies have broad, lasting adoption.

Common technology stack mistakes

First, choosing trendy tools nobody on the team knows. Next, building a custom platform when WordPress or Shopify would do. Then, mixing too many languages and services. After that, ignoring hosting and maintenance costs. Finally, locking data into a platform with no export path.
