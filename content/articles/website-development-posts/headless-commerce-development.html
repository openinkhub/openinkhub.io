Headless commerce development separates an online store's front end, the storefront customers see, from the ecommerce backend that manages products, carts, orders and payments. The two communicate through APIs. As a result, you can build a very fast, fully custom storefront, sell across websites, apps and other channels, and change parts of the system without rebuilding everything. It costs more than a theme-based store, so it suits growing brands with clear needs for speed, flexibility or many channels.

How headless commerce works

  • Commerce backend: Shopify, BigCommerce, Medusa, commercetools or a WooCommerce API.
  • Front end: usually Next.js or another modern framework.
  • Content: a headless CMS for landing pages, blogs and campaigns.
  • Services: search, reviews, payments and analytics connected by API.

This modular approach is often called composable commerce. Our guide to the headless CMS explains the content side.

When headless commerce development makes sense

  1. Your store is growing and theme limits slow down marketing.
  2. Speed and Core Web Vitals are directly hurting conversions.
  3. You sell through a website, app, kiosks or marketplaces from one backend.
  4. You need complex content, personalisation or multiple regions.
  5. You have developers, in-house or through a partner, for ongoing work.

If none of these apply, a well-built Shopify or WooCommerce store is usually the better investment. See our guide to the best ecommerce platform.

The development process

A typical project starts with discovery and architecture, choosing the commerce backend, CMS and front end. Next comes design, then building the storefront, cart and checkout flows, followed by integrations such as payments, shipping and ERP. After that, performance and SEO testing, then a careful launch with redirects. Shopify's headless documentation shows one common setup.

What headless commerce costs

In India, headless projects often start around ₹5 lakh to ₹8 lakh and rise with integrations, custom features and the number of channels. In addition, there are running costs: commerce platform fees, CMS plans, hosting and developer time for changes. Our ecommerce website development cost guide covers traditional options for comparison.

Common mistakes

First, going headless for a small catalogue that a theme would serve well. Next, forgetting that marketers now need a CMS to edit pages. Then, rebuilding checkout from scratch when the platform's checkout is proven. Finally, launching without SEO checks for rendering, metadata and redirects.

Choosing a headless commerce partner

A headless commerce company should show live headless storefronts and explain why each project needed headless. In addition, ask how they keep checkout reliable, how marketers will edit content and how they measure Core Web Vitals after launch. Finally, check that they can maintain the store month to month, because headless ecommerce needs ongoing developer support rather than a one-time build.
