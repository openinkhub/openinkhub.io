WordPress vs Webflow is a choice between flexibility and visual control. WordPress is open-source, self-hosted and excellent for content, SEO, ecommerce and ownership. Webflow is a hosted visual builder that gives designers pixel-level control and fast hosting without plugins. Choose WordPress for blogs, stores and content-heavy sites. Choose Webflow for design-led marketing sites run by a small team without developers.

Cost

WordPress software is free, but you pay for hosting, premium plugins, a theme and maintenance. Webflow charges monthly site plans, and teams may also need workspace plans. Webflow's pricing includes hosting and security, so there is less to manage. However, costs rise with CMS items, traffic and features. Check current plans on Webflow's pricing page and compare against our WordPress website cost guide.

SEO

Both platforms handle the technical basics well: custom titles, descriptions, clean URLs, sitemaps and redirects. Webflow produces tidy code and fast hosting by default. WordPress offers deeper SEO tooling, more schema options and a stronger editor for publishing at scale. Therefore, for a site that will grow through lots of content, WordPress usually has the edge.

Design and editing

Webflow is a designer's tool. It lets you build layouts and animations visually, with output close to hand-written CSS. WordPress design depends on the theme and blocks you use. A custom WordPress theme can match any design, but it needs a developer. On the editing side, writers often prefer WordPress's block editor, while designers prefer Webflow's canvas.

Flexibility and integrations

WordPress has thousands of plugins for bookings, memberships, multilingual content, forms and ecommerce. Webflow has fewer options, so complex features may need third-party tools or custom code. In addition, WordPress can be extended with custom PHP, while Webflow's custom code is more limited.

Maintenance and security

Webflow handles hosting, updates and security, which suits small teams. WordPress needs updates, backups and security checks, usually through a maintenance plan. That is extra work, but it also gives you full control and ownership.

Which should you choose?

  • Choose WordPress for blogs, content-led SEO, WooCommerce stores, complex features or full ownership.
  • Choose Webflow for design-led marketing sites, landing pages and teams without developers.

Common platform mistakes

First, picking a platform because a designer prefers it, not because it fits the business. Next, ignoring content plans: a site that will publish hundreds of articles needs strong content tools. Then, forgetting the cost of moving later, because migrating off any platform takes work. Finally, not checking who will edit the site day to day.
