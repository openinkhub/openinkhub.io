The ecommerce website development process runs through seven steps: planning, platform choice, design, catalog setup, payments and shipping, testing, and launch with ongoing improvement. A simple store can move through these in four to eight weeks. A store with custom features, a large catalog or ERP integration usually takes three to six months. Knowing the steps helps you prepare and keeps the project on schedule.

Step 1: planning and scope

Start with the business, not the design. Who are your customers? How many products will you sell at launch? Which cities will you ship to? Will you offer cash on delivery? Then list the features you need now and those that can wait. The result is a short scope document, a sitemap and a budget. This step prevents most of the costly changes that happen later.

Step 2: choosing the platform

Next, pick the platform that fits the scope. Shopify suits fast launches with little maintenance. WooCommerce suits content-led stores that want control. Custom or headless builds suit complex needs. Our comparison of Shopify vs WooCommerce explains the trade-offs.

Step 3: UX and visual design

Designers map the shopper's path: home, category, product, cart and checkout. Wireframes come first, then visual designs for mobile and desktop. Focus on the product page and checkout, because they decide sales. For example, a large "Add to cart" button and a clear delivery estimate often matter more than a fancy home page banner.

Step 4: development and catalog setup

Developers build the theme or front end, set up categories, filters and search, and import products. Large catalogs are usually uploaded from a spreadsheet with names, prices, variants, stock and images. Meanwhile, your team should write or review product descriptions. Unique, helpful descriptions improve both sales and SEO.

Step 5: payments, shipping and integrations

  • Payments: connect a gateway such as Razorpay or PayU, plus UPI and COD.
  • Shipping: set zones, rates and courier integration with tracking.
  • Taxes: configure GST and invoices.
  • Integrations: link inventory, ERP, CRM, email and WhatsApp updates if needed.
  • Analytics: add Google Analytics and conversion tracking.

Step 6: testing

Test everything a shopper might do. Place real orders with every payment method, including failed payments and refunds. Check shipping rates for different PIN codes, coupons, stock limits and emails. Then test on several phones and browsers. Finally, check page speed, because slow stores lose buyers. Google's PageSpeed Insights is a quick way to measure it.

Step 7: launch and growth

At launch, submit the sitemap to Google Search Console, set up redirects if you are moving from an old store, and watch orders closely for the first week. After that, improve the store based on data: which products sell, where shoppers drop off and which campaigns convert. Our guide to ecommerce conversion optimization lists practical next steps.

What you should prepare

  1. Product list with prices, variants and stock.
  2. Good product photos, ideally on a plain background.
  3. Shipping rules and return policy.
  4. Payment gateway account and business documents.
  5. Logo, brand colours and any brand guidelines.

Having these ready can cut weeks from the timeline.
