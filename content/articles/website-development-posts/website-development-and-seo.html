Website development and SEO are tightly linked. The way a site is built decides how easily search engines can crawl, render and understand its pages, and how fast and usable those pages feel to visitors. Site structure, page speed, mobile design, JavaScript rendering, HTTPS, structured data and internal links are all development decisions. Get them right at build time and content has a fair chance to rank. Get them wrong and even excellent content struggles.

The technical factors that matter most

Crawlable structure

Use real links with href attributes, logical URLs and a clear hierarchy, so every important page is a few clicks from the home page. Our guide to website architecture covers this in depth.

Rendering

Public pages should arrive as complete HTML, through server-side rendering, static generation or a traditional CMS. Pages that depend entirely on browser JavaScript can be indexed slowly or partly. See JavaScript SEO.

Speed

Optimised images, light code, caching and a CDN keep pages fast. Our Core Web Vitals optimization guide explains the metrics.

Mobile-first

Design for phones first, with readable text, tappable buttons and no content hidden on mobile.

Metadata and schema

Every page needs a unique title, description and canonical URL, plus structured data where it fits, such as articles, products, FAQs and local business details.

What SEO-friendly development costs

Building SEO in from the start adds very little to a project, because it is mostly about making good choices. Fixing a poorly built site later, for example re-platforming a JavaScript-only site or untangling thousands of duplicate URLs, can cost far more than the original build. So include SEO in the brief, not after launch.

How to test your site's SEO

  • Google Search Console: indexing, coverage, Core Web Vitals and errors.
  • URL Inspection: see the rendered HTML Google actually gets.
  • PageSpeed Insights: lab and real-user speed data.
  • A crawler: find broken links, redirect chains and duplicates.
  • Rich Results Test: check structured data.

Google's SEO starter guide is a good companion.

Common SEO development mistakes

First, launching a redesign without 301 redirects, which loses years of rankings. Next, blocking the staging site's noindex setting into production. Then, heavy page builders and scripts that slow every page. After that, duplicate URLs from filters and parameters. Finally, content that only loads after a click or scroll.

Platforms and SEO

WordPress, Shopify, Next.js and custom builds can all rank well when built carefully. The platform matters less than the decisions made during development.
