A website maintenance checklist keeps your site secure, fast and accurate without last-minute emergencies. The essentials are software updates, backups, security scans, uptime monitoring, speed checks, broken link and form tests, content reviews, SEO checks and renewals of your domain, hosting and certificates. Some tasks need doing weekly, others monthly, quarterly or once a year. Below is a practical schedule you can follow yourself or hand to a maintenance partner.

Weekly tasks

  • Check that the site loads and key pages work.
  • Confirm automatic backups ran successfully.
  • Apply security updates for the CMS, themes and plugins.
  • Review security alerts, failed logins and malware scan results.
  • Test the main contact or enquiry form.
  • Moderate comments and clear form spam.

Monthly tasks

  • Update all plugins, themes and extensions after testing on staging.
  • Run a speed test on top pages and check Core Web Vitals.
  • Scan for broken links and fix them.
  • Review Google Search Console for errors and indexing issues.
  • Check analytics for unusual drops in traffic or conversions.
  • Remove unused plugins, users and old drafts.
  • Test a backup restore on a staging copy every few months.

Quarterly tasks

  • Refresh key content: prices, team, services, contact details.
  • Update older blog posts with new information and internal links.
  • Review SEO titles and descriptions on important pages.
  • Test the site on several phones and browsers.
  • Check accessibility basics: alt text, contrast, keyboard use.
  • Review user accounts and access levels.

Yearly tasks

  • Renew the domain, hosting, SSL certificate and premium licences.
  • Review hosting performance and plan upgrades.
  • Audit the whole site's design, structure and goals.
  • Update legal pages such as privacy and terms.
  • Decide whether a refresh or redesign is due; see website redesign cost.

What you can do yourself

Content updates, form tests, reading analytics and spotting broken pages are easy for most teams. Updates, backups, security and speed work are safer with an expert, because a bad update can break a site. WordPress's own update guide explains why testing and backups come first.

Common maintenance mistakes

First, updating everything on the live site without a backup. Next, backups that were never tested. Then, ignoring Search Console warnings. After that, letting the domain expire because the renewal email went to an old address. Finally, never removing old plugins and admin accounts.

Keeping a maintenance log

Finally, record every update, fix and check in a simple log. For example, note the date, what changed and who did it. As a result, when something breaks, you can quickly see what changed last, and handing the website to a new team becomes much easier.
