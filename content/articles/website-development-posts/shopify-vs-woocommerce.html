Shopify vs WooCommerce is the most common platform choice for a new online store. Shopify is a hosted, all-in-one platform: you pay a monthly fee and get hosting, security and checkout built in. WooCommerce is a free plugin for WordPress: you own and run the whole store, so you get more control but also more responsibility. In short, choose Shopify for speed and simplicity, and WooCommerce for flexibility and content-led selling.

Cost: launch vs long term

Shopify is usually cheaper to launch, because you skip hosting and server setup. However, its costs add up over time: the monthly plan, paid apps and, unless you use Shopify Payments where available, transaction fees. WooCommerce has no platform fee. Instead, you pay for hosting, premium plugins and someone to keep it updated. For a store with steady sales and many features, WooCommerce often costs less over three years. Features and flexibility

Both platforms cover the basics well: product pages, variants, inventory, coupons, shipping rules and order management. The difference is in how far you can go. With Shopify, unusual features come from apps, and some things simply cannot be changed. With WooCommerce, a developer can build almost anything, such as custom pricing rules, B2B ordering or complex product options. On the other hand, more freedom means more ways to slow the site down if plugins are chosen badly.

SEO and content

Both can rank well on Google. Shopify handles technical basics automatically but fixes some URL patterns, such as /products/ and /collections/. WooCommerce gives full control over URLs, schema and content, and it sits on WordPress, which is still the strongest blogging tool. So if content marketing is central to your plan, WooCommerce has the edge. Our website SEO and performance page explains what matters most.

Payments in India

Indian shoppers expect UPI, cards, net banking, wallets and often cash on delivery. Both platforms support Indian gateways such as Razorpay, PayU and Cashfree through apps or plugins. Check the gateway's fees and settlement times, because they affect cash flow more than the platform does.

Maintenance and security

Shopify looks after hosting, updates and security for you. That is a big advantage for small teams. With WooCommerce, you must update WordPress, the theme and plugins, keep backups and watch security. Many stores solve this with a monthly maintenance plan. Scaling a growing store

Shopify scales smoothly for traffic, because Shopify runs the servers. WooCommerce scales well too, but it needs good hosting, caching and a clean setup. For very large or complex catalogs, both can be extended, and some brands move to headless commerce later.

Common migration mistakes

  • Changing product URLs without 301 redirects, which loses rankings.
  • Forgetting customer accounts and order history.
  • Not testing payment and shipping rules before launch.
  • Dropping product reviews and structured data.

Which should you choose?

Pick Shopify if you want to launch quickly, have a small team and value low maintenance. Pick WooCommerce if you want full control, already use WordPress or plan to grow through content.
