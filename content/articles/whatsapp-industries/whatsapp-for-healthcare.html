Booking appointments without phone queues

The front desk of a busy clinic spends hours on the phone. Most calls are simple: "Is the doctor in today?" or "Can I get a slot on Friday?" A WhatsApp booking flow handles these well:

  1. The patient chooses a department or doctor.
  2. The flow shows open dates and time slots.
  3. The patient picks one and shares their name and age.
  4. The booking goes into your calendar or hospital system.
  5. A confirmation with the address and map link goes out straight away.

Reminders that cut no-shows

Missed appointments waste doctor time. A reminder the day before and another two hours before the slot helps a lot. Add buttons for "Confirm", "Reschedule" and "Cancel", so the slot can go to someone else if needed. Similarly, send reminders for vaccinations, follow-up visits and medicine refills. Each one is a utility message linked to an existing booking or treatment.

Sharing reports safely

Patients love getting lab results on their phone. However, health data needs care. Rather than attaching the report itself, send a secure link that asks for a date of birth or one-time code. Only send results to the number the patient registered, and only after they agree to receive them on WhatsApp. Our view is simple: convenience should never come at the cost of privacy.

Follow-ups, feedback and reviews

  • Post-visit check-in: "How are you feeling after your procedure?" with a button to speak to a nurse.
  • Medicine adherence: gentle reminders for long courses.
  • Feedback: a one-tap rating after each visit.
  • Reviews: a link to your Google profile for happy patients.

What the chatbot should not do

A healthcare chatbot must not diagnose, prescribe or give medical advice. Instead, it should handle bookings, timings, fees, directions and general information. When a patient describes symptoms, the bot should pass the chat to qualified staff, or advise them to call emergency services if it sounds urgent. Set these rules before launch and test them carefully.

Privacy rules for clinics

First, collect explicit consent before sending health messages. Next, limit who can open chats in the team inbox, by role. Then avoid health details in marketing messages. Finally, follow India's data protection law and any guidance from your medical council. Keep a record of consent with each patient's profile.

Rolling out WhatsApp for healthcare

A good first step is appointment booking plus reminders, because the drop in no-shows is easy to measure. After that, add report links, follow-ups and feedback.
