WhatsApp for events and webinars helps organisers fill seats and get people to actually show up. Registrations can happen in the chat, joining links and venue maps arrive on the attendee's phone, and reminders reach people who would never open a calendar email. After the event, replays, certificates and feedback requests go out on the same thread. For conferences, workshops, college fests and online webinars, the result is usually higher attendance.

The attendance gap

Most organisers know the pattern: many sign up, far fewer attend. People register on impulse, then forget. Email reminders land in promotions folders. However, a WhatsApp reminder appears on the lock screen. That is why moving reminders to WhatsApp is often the single biggest change an event team can make.

Taking registrations in the chat

Instead of a long web form, registration can happen inside WhatsApp. A click-to-WhatsApp ad or a QR code on a poster opens the chat. Then a short flow asks for name, email, company or college, and session choice. For paid events, it sends a payment link and confirms once paid. WhatsApp Flows can also show a neat form inside the chat. See our chatbot flow builder guide for how these work.

Reminders and joining links

Timing matters. A simple schedule that works for many webinars is:

  1. On registration: confirmation with date, time and an "Add to calendar" link.
  2. One day before: what to expect and the speaker line-up.
  3. One hour before: the joining link.
  4. At start time: "We are live now" with the link again.

For in-person events, swap the joining link for a map pin, parking details and gate timings. These messages relate to a registration, so they are utility templates in most cases.

Tickets and check-in

Send each attendee a ticket with a QR code as an image or link. At the venue, staff scan it from the attendee's phone. No printing, no lost emails. In addition, last-minute changes, such as a hall switch, can reach everyone in minutes through WhatsApp broadcast messaging.

During the event

Attendees can message questions for the speaker, request help at the venue or ask about the next session. A bot answers the common ones, such as "Where is the lunch area?" or "When does the panel start?", and the team handles the rest.

Replays, certificates and follow-up

After the event, send:

  • the recording link for those who missed it;
  • slides or resources promised during the session;
  • participation certificates for workshops;
  • an invitation to the next event.

For B2B webinars, this is also when sales should follow up. Hand warm leads to the team with their questions and attendance time attached, through CRM integration.

Collecting feedback

Feedback forms by email get few replies. On WhatsApp, a single question with buttons, such as "How would you rate today's session? 1 to 5", gets many more. Then ask one open question: "What should we cover next time?" Short and quick wins here.
