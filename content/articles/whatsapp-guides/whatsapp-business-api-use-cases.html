WhatsApp Business API use cases fall into four groups: sales, support, marketing and operations. Sales teams use it to capture and qualify leads. Support teams use it to answer questions and resolve issues. Marketing teams use it for campaigns and win-back messages. Operations teams use it for order, booking and payment updates. Most businesses start with one group and expand once the first use case pays off.

Sales use cases

  • Click-to-WhatsApp ads: ads that open a chat, so leads arrive ready to talk.
  • Instant lead replies: website and portal leads get a WhatsApp message within seconds.
  • Lead qualification: a bot asks budget, timeline and needs, then scores the lead.
  • Demo and visit booking: slots offered and confirmed in the chat.
  • Quotes and proposals: PDFs and price lists shared, with follow-ups.

For real examples by sector, see WhatsApp for real estate and WhatsApp for automobile dealers.

Support use cases

  • FAQ chatbot: answers timings, policies and prices any time.
  • Ticket creation: a message opens a ticket in your helpdesk.
  • Order help: "where is my order?" answered from your store data.
  • Returns and exchanges: a guided flow with photo upload.
  • Live chat hand-off: complex cases routed to agents with full history.
  • Voice calls: customers call from the chat when typing is not enough.

The team inbox and AI bot guides explain how these fit together.

Marketing use cases

  • Segmented campaigns: offers sent to the right group of opted-in customers.
  • Cart recovery: reminders for abandoned checkouts.
  • Back-in-stock and price-drop alerts.
  • Win-back: messages for lapsed customers.
  • Event and webinar invitations with reminders.
  • Review and referral requests after a good experience.

Operations use cases

  • Order lifecycle: confirmed, packed, shipped, delivered.
  • COD confirmation: verify cash orders before dispatch.
  • Appointment reminders with confirm and reschedule buttons.
  • Payment reminders with secure links, plus receipts.
  • Document collection: KYC, forms and photos.
  • One-time passcodes: login and verification codes.
  • Service alerts: outages, delays and schedule changes.

Choosing where to start

Pick the use case with the most volume and the clearest result. Transactional updates are often the easiest win, because customers expect them and they rarely cause blocks. Next, add a bot for common questions. After that, move to sales and marketing journeys. Our WhatsApp automation guide has a simple scoring method.

Measuring each use case

Every use case needs one number that shows whether it works. For sales, track leads qualified and meetings booked. For support, track first response time and the share of chats the bot resolves. Marketing campaigns live or die on replies, clicks and orders. Operations messages are judged by fewer calls to your team, such as fewer "where is my order?" questions. Review these monthly, and drop or rework any use case that does not move its number. Our WhatsApp analytics guide explains how to track them.
