The WhatsApp statistics that matter most for businesses are simple: WhatsApp has more than 3 billion monthly users worldwide, India is its largest market, and more than 200 million businesses use WhatsApp Business tools. Those numbers explain why customers expect to reach brands on WhatsApp. Beyond the headline figures, though, many widely shared statistics have weak sources. This page sticks to figures with clear origins and shows how to track your own.

Global WhatsApp user numbers

  • More than 3 billion monthly users. Meta announced that WhatsApp passed 3 billion monthly users in 2025.
  • Available in 180+ countries and 60+ languages, according to WhatsApp.
  • Part of a larger family. Meta reports more than 3 billion daily users across its apps combined, in its quarterly investor results.

Meta updates these figures from time to time, so always check the latest announcement before quoting them.

WhatsApp users in India

India is WhatsApp's biggest market. Meta's filings and public statements have put Indian users at more than 500 million. In practice, that means most smartphone users in India already have WhatsApp installed. For Indian businesses, that reach is the main reason to invest in the channel. In addition, many Indian customers already use WhatsApp to talk to local shops, so a business chat feels familiar rather than intrusive.

Business use of WhatsApp

  • 200 million+ businesses use the WhatsApp Business App, according to Meta (2023).
  • Per-message pricing since 1 July 2025. Meta moved the WhatsApp Business Platform from conversation-based to per-message pricing. See our pricing guide.
  • Calling and Flows have been added to the Business Platform, so businesses can take calls and collect forms inside chats.

Reading WhatsApp statistics carefully

You will often see claims such as "98% open rates" or "WhatsApp gets 10x the response of email". Treat these with caution. Many come from vendor marketing, small samples or old studies. Open rates also vary by country, audience, message type and whether read receipts are on. So before you rely on any statistic, check three things:

  1. Who published it? Meta, an independent researcher, or a vendor selling a tool?
  2. When? WhatsApp has changed a lot since 2020.
  3. Where? A figure from Brazil may not hold for India.

In general, it is fair to say WhatsApp messages are read faster and more often than marketing emails. However, the exact gap depends on your audience.

Measuring your own WhatsApp numbers

Your own data beats any industry average. Track these from the first campaign:

  • delivery, read and reply rates per template;
  • click-through rate on buttons and links;
  • conversion rate and revenue per message;
  • block and opt-out rates;
  • first response time for support chats.

After three months, you will have a baseline that is far more useful than any published figure. Our WhatsApp analytics guide explains each metric.
