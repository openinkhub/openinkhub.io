A WhatsApp API provider, often called a Business Solution Provider or BSP, gives your business access to the WhatsApp Business Platform plus the tools to use it: a shared inbox, chatbot builder, campaigns and reports. Choosing the right WhatsApp API provider affects your monthly cost, what you can automate and how easily you can leave later. This checklist covers what to compare before you sign.

What a WhatsApp API provider does

  • Helps with Meta business verification, number registration and display name approval.
  • Hosts or connects to the Cloud API on your behalf.
  • Provides software: team inbox, chatbot, broadcasts, templates and analytics.
  • Offers integrations with CRMs, stores and helpdesks.
  • Gives support when something breaks.

Going direct vs using a provider

Since Meta launched the Cloud API, any business can connect directly. However, direct access gives you only the raw API. You would still need to build the inbox, bot and reporting yourself. That makes sense for companies with strong developer teams. For most businesses, a provider or an implementation partner is faster and cheaper overall.

What to compare

  1. Meta partner status. Check that the provider is listed in Meta's partner directory.
  2. Features you will actually use. Inbox, AI bot, flow builder, campaigns, catalog, calling and reports.
  3. Integrations. Does it connect to your CRM, store and helpdesk without custom work?
  4. Agent seats. Is there a per-user fee? How many agents are included?
  5. Limits. Contacts, messages, bots or workflows capped by plan.
  6. Support. Hours, language, response times and an Indian support team if you need one.
  7. Data ownership. Can you export contacts, templates and chat history at any time?
  8. Exit terms. Notice period, lock-in and help with moving your number.

Pricing questions to ask

Provider pricing is often hard to compare. So ask every provider the same questions, in writing:

  • What is Meta's charge per message, and what is your markup, shown separately?
  • What is the monthly platform fee, and what does it include?
  • Are there setup fees or charges for extra numbers?
  • Do you charge for free service conversations?
  • Is the AI bot billed separately, per message or per seat?

Then model your expected monthly volume across each provider. Our per-message pricing guide explains Meta's side of the bill.

Red flags

  • Promises of "unlimited free messages" or sending without templates.
  • "No opt-in needed" or offers to send to purchased lists.
  • Unofficial or "WhatsApp Web" based tools sold as API access.
  • The provider owning your WhatsApp Business Account instead of you.
  • No way to export your data.

Any of these can lead to a banned number. Our guide on bulk WhatsApp messages explains why unofficial tools are risky.

A quick decision checklist

Before signing, confirm that: your business portfolio owns the account; Meta and provider fees are shown separately; the features you need are on the plan you are buying; your CRM connects; and you can export data and move your number. If all five are true, you are in good shape.
