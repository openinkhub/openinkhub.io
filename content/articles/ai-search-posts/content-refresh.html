A content refresh is a planned update of an existing page to make it more accurate, more useful and more competitive, while keeping the same URL. Refresh a page when its traffic or rankings decline, when facts or prices change, or when the top results now answer the question better. A careful content refresh often lifts results faster than publishing something new.

Signs a page needs a content refresh

  • Clicks or impressions in Search Console have fallen for three months or more.
  • The page has slipped from the top five to lower positions.
  • Facts, prices, laws, screenshots or tools in the article are out of date.
  • Competitors now cover subtopics you skip.
  • The search results show a new format, such as video, lists or AI Overviews.
  • Readers leave quickly or comments point out errors.

How to pick pages to refresh first

Export pages from Search Console with clicks and impressions for the last 6 months and the 6 months before. Then sort by the biggest drop in clicks. Next, add business value: a falling service page matters more than a falling news post. Finally, start with pages that still rank on page one or two, because they recover fastest.

A step-by-step process

  1. Check the intent. Search the main query and see what ranks now. Has the intent shifted?
  2. Review the content. Mark outdated facts, missing subtopics and weak sections.
  3. Update facts and examples. Replace old figures with current, sourced ones.
  4. Add what is missing. Cover new questions from People Also Ask and Search Console.
  5. Tighten the opening. Put a direct answer in the first 60 words, as in answer-first content.
  6. Improve the title and description. See our title tag formulas.
  7. Refresh links. Fix broken links and add links to newer related pages.
  8. Update the modified date. Only after making real changes.
  9. Request indexing. Use URL Inspection in Search Console to ask Google to recrawl.

Keep rankings safe

Keep the URL the same, since it holds the page's links and history. Avoid removing sections that still rank for useful queries; check the page's queries in Search Console before cutting anything. In addition, keep the main keyword in the title and H1 unless the intent has changed. Big rewrites are fine, but make them on purpose.

How often to refresh

Review key pages every 6 to 12 months. Fast-moving topics, such as AI search or prices, may need a quarterly check. Evergreen guides may only need a yearly look. Meanwhile, set a reminder in your content calendar, so updates do not depend on noticing a drop.

When to merge instead

If two older posts cover the same topic and both are weak, merge them into one stronger page and redirect the other. That often works better than refreshing both. Our guide to keyword cannibalization explains how to decide.

Content refresh and AI answers

AI assistants favour current, specific information. An updated page with fresh dates, new figures and clear answers has a better chance of being cited than an old one. So a content refresh helps both rankings and AI visibility. Google's guidance on publication dates explains how to show dates correctly.

Track the results

Note the refresh date in a sheet, then compare clicks, impressions and position 4 and 8 weeks later. Pages that improve confirm your process; pages that do not may need a bigger rethink.
