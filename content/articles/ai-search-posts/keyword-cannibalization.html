Keyword cannibalization happens when two or more pages on your site target the same search intent, so they compete with each other and neither ranks as well as one strong page would. You can spot it in Search Console when different URLs swap positions for the same query. The usual fixes are to merge the pages, refocus one of them, or make the stronger page clearly the main one with internal links and canonicals.

When it is a real problem

Not every overlap is harmful. If two pages rank for one keyword but serve different intents, such as a guide and a service page, both may do fine. Keyword cannibalization becomes a problem when:

  • Rankings for a query swap back and forth between two URLs.
  • Both pages sit on page two, while a competitor holds the top spot.
  • Links and engagement are split across near-duplicate pages.
  • Google shows the weaker page instead of the one you want.

How to find competing pages

  1. In Search Console, open the Performance report and filter by a target query.
  2. Click the Pages tab to see every URL that gets impressions for it.
  3. If two or more URLs share impressions over time, note them.
  4. Then search site:yourdomain.com keyword on Google to see what else exists.
  5. Check your content list for posts with very similar titles.

A spreadsheet with query, URLs, clicks and positions makes the pattern clear.

Five ways to fix keyword cannibalization

  1. Merge and redirect. Combine the best parts of both pages into the stronger URL, then add a 301 redirect from the weaker one. This is the most common fix.
  2. Refocus one page. Change the weaker page to target a different, related intent, with a new title, headings and angle.
  3. Strengthen internal links. Point internal links with descriptive anchors to the page you want to rank; see our internal linking strategy.
  4. Use a canonical tag. If both pages must stay, such as filtered product views, set the canonical on the duplicate to the main page.
  5. Noindex or remove. For thin pages with no traffic or links, remove them or add noindex.

How to merge safely

First, check both pages' queries and backlinks, so you keep what works. Next, write the merged page with the strongest sections from each. Then add the redirect and update internal links to point straight at the new URL. Finally, watch rankings for four to eight weeks. Our guide to content refresh covers the update process.

Prevent keyword cannibalization

  • Keep a content map that lists one main topic per URL.
  • Check the map before writing any new page.
  • Plan topic clusters so each page has a distinct question; see topical authority.
  • Review the map every quarter as the site grows.

Cannibalization in AI search

When several of your pages say slightly different things on one topic, AI tools may pick the weaker or older version, or get confused about your position. A single, clear, current page gives them one reliable source. As a result, fixing cannibalization helps AI visibility as well as rankings.
