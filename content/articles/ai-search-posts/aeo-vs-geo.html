AEO vs GEO: answer engine optimization (AEO) helps your page become the direct answer in snippets, voice replies and AI summaries, while generative engine optimization (GEO) helps your brand get named and cited inside answers that tools like ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity write. They overlap, but they are not the same job.

Think of it this way. AEO works at the level of a page and a question. GEO works at the level of your whole brand and how AI models understand it across the web.

Where AEO and GEO overlap

Both depend on pages that search engines can crawl, index and trust. Both reward clear, specific answers. And both suffer when a site is slow, thin or full of contradictions. So a page you improve for AEO, with a direct answer and tidy headings, also becomes easier for a generative engine to quote.

That overlap is why most teams should not split AEO and GEO into separate projects. Our guide to the SEO, AEO, GEO and AIO difference shows how the four layers stack.

Where AEO vs GEO differ

AEO is mostly on-page. You control the outcome by editing your own content: lead with answers, use question headings, add tables and FAQs. Our article on what AEO is covers that toolkit.

GEO, however, reaches beyond your site. AI models form a view of your brand from reviews, forums, news, directories and other sites that mention you. As a result, GEO work includes digital PR, consistent business details, original research and community presence. Read what GEO is for the full picture.

Measurement differs too. For AEO you can count snippets and question queries in Search Console. For GEO you need a fixed set of prompts that you run in AI tools each month, as described in our guide to building an AI prompt set.

Which should you start with?

Start with AEO if you already have pages ranking on page one. The fixes are fast, cheap and visible, and they lay groundwork for GEO. Then add GEO once your key pages answer questions well, because there is little point earning brand mentions that lead to weak pages.

There are exceptions. If AI tools describe your brand wrongly, or name competitors for every buyer question, begin GEO work early. Fixing your facts across the web takes time, so it pays to start soon.

How to run AEO and GEO as one plan

  1. List the 30 to 50 questions buyers ask before they buy.
  2. Map each question to one page, and rewrite that page answer-first.
  3. Check that your name, address, services and founders match everywhere online.
  4. Publish one piece of original data or expert insight each quarter.
  5. Track snippets, question queries and AI mentions in one monthly report.
