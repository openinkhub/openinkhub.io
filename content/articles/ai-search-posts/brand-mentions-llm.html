Brand mentions in LLM answers come from two places: the pages an AI tool fetches when it searches the web, and the patterns a large language model learned during training. To grow them, make your brand easy to connect with your topic across many trusted sources, keep your facts consistent and give people genuine reasons to talk about you. In short, brand mentions, LLM visibility and trust tend to rise together.

Why AI tools name some brands and not others

An LLM tends to mention brands that appear often, in context and in agreement across the web. For example, if dozens of articles, reviews and forum threads link "Brand X" with "accounting software for small firms", the model learns that link. In contrast, a brand that only describes itself on its own site leaves little trace.

Live retrieval works in a similar way. When ChatGPT or Perplexity searches, the pages it finds shape the answer. So for brand mentions, LLM answers depend on both your own pages and what others publish about you.

Do unlinked mentions count?

For SEO, a link passes signals that a plain mention does not. However, a language model reads text, so an unlinked mention still connects your brand to a topic. That makes unlinked mentions far more valuable for GEO than they were for classic SEO. Still, aim for links where they fit naturally, since they help both.

How to check your current brand mentions

Run a fixed list of buyer prompts in ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Copilot. Note whether you appear and how you are described. Then ask each tool directly, "What is [your brand] known for?". Inaccurate or thin answers show where to focus. Our guide to the AI prompt set gives a template.

What if AI describes you wrongly?

First, find the source of the error. It might be an old directory listing, an outdated press article or a competitor's comparison page. Correct the facts where you can, then publish a clear "About" page and service pages with the right details. Answers based on live search can update within weeks, while descriptions learned in training change more slowly.

Mistakes that backfire

  • Spamming forums with promotional posts.
  • Buying fake reviews or paid lists that hide sponsorship.
  • Publishing self-praising press releases with no news value.

These tactics damage trust with people, platforms and search engines alike. Google's spam policies cover several of them.
