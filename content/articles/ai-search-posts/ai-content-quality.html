AI content quality comes down to one question: does each page help a real reader, however it was made? Google does not ban AI-generated content. Instead, its spam policies target scaled content abuse, which means producing many pages mainly to manipulate rankings, with little value for users. So a clear AI content policy and a human review step for every page protect both your rankings and your reputation.

What Google's policy says

In March 2024, Google added a "scaled content abuse" section to its spam policies. It covers content made at scale, whether by AI, people or both, where the main purpose is ranking rather than helping. Google's guidance on AI content also says that using automation is fine when the result is helpful and original.

In short, the method is not the problem. Thin, repetitive, unreviewed pages are.

Signs of low AI content quality

  • Many pages that swap one keyword into the same text.
  • Generic advice with no examples, data or experience.
  • Facts, prices or laws that are wrong or out of date.
  • Repeated phrases and filler that add no meaning.
  • No named author or reviewer.
  • Claims about services the business does not offer.

A simple AI content policy template

Write a one-page policy and share it with everyone who creates content. It can cover:

  1. Allowed uses: research, outlines, first drafts, editing and translation.
  2. Not allowed: publishing unreviewed AI drafts, inventing statistics, fake reviews or fake quotes.
  3. Review: a subject expert checks facts and adds real experience before publishing.
  4. Sources: every statistic links to its original source.
  5. Disclosure: a short note explains how the team uses AI, where that helps readers.
  6. Volume: new page batches start small and grow only when they perform.
  7. Ownership: one editor approves every page and owns updates.

Review checklist for every page

  • Does the page answer the reader's question better than the current top results?
  • Are all facts, figures and dates correct and sourced?
  • Does it include something only we can add, such as experience, data or examples?
  • Is it free of filler, repeats and vague claims?
  • Does it match what we actually sell and do?
  • Is the author or reviewer named, with a short bio?

Scaled content review for existing sites

If you already published many AI-assisted pages, audit them. First, export all URLs with their traffic and indexing status. Next, flag pages with near-identical text, no traffic or thin content. Then decide for each: improve, merge or remove. Merging weak pages into stronger ones often lifts the whole section. Our guide to keyword cannibalization explains how to merge safely.

AI content quality and AI search

AI assistants also prefer sources that are clear, specific and trustworthy. So the same quality bar that protects your rankings also raises your odds of being cited.
