A good title tag is about 50 to 60 characters long, puts the main keyword and the main benefit near the start, and describes the page accurately. A good meta description is about 70 to 160 characters, summarises what the reader gets and gives a reason to click. Simple formulas for each page type make both quick to write and consistent across your site.

Why title tags still matter

The title tag is one of the clearest signals of what a page is about, and it is usually the blue headline people see in search results. A clear title raises click-through rate, while a vague one gets ignored. Google may rewrite titles that are too long, stuffed or misleading, so accurate titles are more likely to show as written. Google explains its approach in its guide to title links.

Meta description formulas

  • Problem + solution + action: "Site not on Google? Our 10-point audit finds the cause in 48 hours. Get your free report."
  • Answer + detail: "Local SEO usually takes 3 to 6 months. See the steps, costs and quick wins that speed it up."
  • Offer + proof: "Monthly SEO plans from ₹10,000. Clear reports, no lock-in, and a team that answers on WhatsApp."

Meta descriptions do not affect rankings directly, but they shape clicks. Also, Google sometimes shows the description as the snippet, so make it accurate.

Rules for both

  1. Write a unique title tag and description for every page.
  2. Put the most important words first, since long titles get cut.
  3. Match the page content exactly; no bait.
  4. Use the reader's words, not internal jargon.
  5. Avoid stuffing several keyword variants into one title.
  6. Add the brand name at the end when space allows.

How to test and improve

In Search Console, find pages with high impressions and low click-through rate. Those titles are your best candidates. Rewrite them with a clearer benefit, then compare clicks over the next four weeks. Meanwhile, check how Google displays each title by searching for the page; if it rewrites yours, the new version often hints at what it wants.

Title tags and AI search

AI tools read titles and headings to understand pages quickly. So a clear title and H1 that match the main question help both search and AI answers. Pair them with an answer-first opening, as described in our guide to answer-first content.

Common mistakes

  • Using the same title on many pages.
  • Leaving default titles such as "Home" or "Untitled".
  • Writing titles over 70 characters that lose the key words.
  • Promising something the page does not deliver.
