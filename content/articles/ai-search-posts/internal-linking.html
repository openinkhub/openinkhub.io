Internal linking connects pages on your own site, and a good strategy for topic clusters links every cluster page to its pillar, the pillar to every cluster page, and close siblings to each other, using anchor text that describes the target. This helps search engines find and understand your pages, spreads authority to the ones that matter and guides readers to the next useful step.

Why internal linking matters

Search engines discover pages by following links. A page with no internal links pointing to it, called an orphan page, may never be found or may be seen as unimportant. In addition, links pass signals: a page linked from many relevant pages looks more central to the topic. For readers, good links answer the natural next question.

The cluster linking model

  1. Pillar to cluster: the pillar page links to every cluster page, usually in the section that summarises each subtopic.
  2. Cluster to pillar: each cluster page links back to the pillar early, often in the first or second paragraph.
  3. Cluster to cluster: related cluster pages link to each other where it helps, two or three links per page.
  4. Service links: informational pages link to the matching service page, so readers can take action.

How to write good anchor text

  • Describe the target page, for example "our guide to Core Web Vitals".
  • Vary the wording naturally instead of repeating one exact phrase.
  • Avoid "click here" and "read more" on their own.
  • Keep anchors short, usually two to six words.
  • Make sure the link fits the sentence and helps the reader.

Where to place internal links

Links inside the main content carry the most weight, because they sit in context. Navigation and footer links help discovery but say less about relevance. So add contextual links in body text wherever a related page would genuinely help. Also, consider a short "related reading" list at the end of articles.

Find pages that need more links

  1. Crawl your site with a tool such as Screaming Frog, or use your CMS reports.
  2. Sort pages by the number of internal links pointing to them.
  3. Flag important pages with few links, plus any orphans.
  4. Search your site for mentions of each topic, then add links from those pages.
  5. Check the Links report in Search Console for a quick count.

How many internal links per page?

There is no fixed rule. Most articles benefit from at least three to five relevant internal links, and long guides may have many more. Instead of counting, ask whether each link helps. Too many links in a short page can dilute focus and annoy readers.

Internal linking and AI search

AI crawlers also follow links, and clear links show which pages cover which subtopics. As a result, well-linked clusters give AI tools a full picture of your expertise. Make sure links are real HTML anchors, not JavaScript actions, as explained in JavaScript SEO for AI.

Common mistakes

  • Linking only from navigation and never from body text.
  • Pointing many links at the home page instead of deep pages.
  • Leaving broken internal links after URL changes.
  • Linking two competing pages for the same keyword, which feeds keyword cannibalization.
