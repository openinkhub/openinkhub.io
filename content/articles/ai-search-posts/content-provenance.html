Content provenance is the record of who created a piece of content, how it was made and where its facts come from. On the web, it shows up as author pages, source lists, edit dates and, increasingly, C2PA content credentials attached to images and video. Clear content provenance helps readers trust you, and it gives search engines and AI tools stronger signals about expertise and authenticity.

Why content provenance matters now

AI tools can produce text and images in seconds, so readers and platforms want to know what is real and who stands behind it. For example, Google's quality rater guidelines stress trust, experience and expertise. Meanwhile, AI assistants prefer sources they can verify. As a result, showing your work has become a practical advantage, not just good manners.

What is C2PA?

C2PA stands for the Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity. In practice, it publishes an open standard for "content credentials": signed information attached to a file that records how it was created and edited. For example, a photo can carry a record of the camera, the editing app and any AI tools used. Also, the C2PA website explains the standard and lists supporting companies.

Several cameras, editing tools and platforms now support content credentials. So if you publish original photos or graphics, check whether your tools can add them.

Author pages that build trust

  1. Create one page per author with a real name and photo.
  2. Then describe their role, experience and areas of expertise in plain words.
  3. List qualifications, certifications or notable projects where relevant.
  4. Link to their LinkedIn profile and other official profiles.
  5. Add Person schema with sameAs links; see our schema markup guide.
  6. Finally, link every article to its author page.

Source transparency on every page

When you quote a statistic, link to the original source, not to a blog that repeated it. Similarly, when you share an opinion, say it is your view. And when facts change, update the page and show a "last updated" date. Readers notice these habits, and so do AI tools that check claims against other sources.

Being open about AI use

If your team uses AI tools for research or drafts, a short note explaining how helps readers. For instance: "We use AI tools to help research and outline articles. A named expert writes, checks and approves every page." This sort of disclosure supports trust without distracting from the content. Plus, our guide to AI content quality includes a policy template.

Edit history and dates

Show a published date and a modified date on articles. Change the modified date only when you make real updates; changing it without new content misleads readers. For key pages, a short change log can also help, especially for pricing or legal topics. Our guide to content refresh explains when updates are worth making.

Content provenance checklist

  • Every article names an author linked to a full author page.
  • Statistics link to their original sources.
  • Published and updated dates appear on each article.
  • Original images carry content credentials once your tools allow it.
  • A short AI use note is available on the site.
  • Organization and Person schema match the visible details.
