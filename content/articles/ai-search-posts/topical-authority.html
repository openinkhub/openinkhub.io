Topical authority is how much search engines trust your site on a subject, earned by covering that subject in depth with helpful, well-linked pages. The usual way to build it is with topic clusters: one pillar page that covers a broad topic, surrounded by cluster pages that each answer a narrower question, all linked together. Sites with strong topical authority tend to rank for more related searches and get cited more often in AI answers.

How topic clusters work

Picture a wheel. The hub is a pillar page, such as "Local SEO: The Complete Guide". The spokes are cluster pages, such as "How to get Google reviews", "Local citations explained" and "Local SEO for clinics". Each cluster page links to the pillar, and the pillar links to each cluster. In addition, related clusters link to each other.

This structure helps search engines see that you cover the whole topic, not just one keyword. It also helps readers move from a broad overview to the detail they need.

How to plan a cluster

  1. Choose a core topic that matches a service you sell.
  2. List the questions buyers ask about it, using the methods in our guide to question research.
  3. Group the questions by intent: learn, compare and buy.
  4. Assign one page per main question, so no two pages compete.
  5. Write the pillar as a broad guide that summarises each subtopic and links to the cluster pages.
  6. Write the cluster pages in depth, each with an answer-first opening.
  7. Link them together with descriptive anchor text.

Linking rules for clusters

Every cluster page links back to the pillar near the top or in the introduction. The pillar links to every cluster page. Meanwhile, cluster pages link to two or three siblings where it helps the reader. Use anchor text that describes the target page, not "click here". Our internal linking strategy guide covers the details.

How many pages do you need?

There is no magic number. A focused cluster of 8 to 15 strong pages often beats 50 thin ones. So cover the questions that matter to buyers first, then expand where search data shows demand. Quality and depth count more than volume.

How to measure topical authority

  • Count the keywords the whole cluster ranks for in Search Console, not just the pillar.
  • Watch whether new cluster pages rank faster than earlier ones.
  • Track featured snippets and AI citations across the cluster.
  • Check internal link coverage so no cluster page is orphaned.

If new pages in a cluster start ranking within weeks rather than months, your topical authority is growing.

Topical authority and AI search

AI tools prefer sources that cover a topic well and consistently. Google's AI Mode also runs several related searches for one question, so a site that answers many sub-questions has more chances to appear. As a result, topic clusters help both rankings and AI visibility. Google's guide to helpful, people-first content describes the depth it looks for.
