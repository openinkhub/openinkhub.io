An AI SEO audit checks how both Google and AI assistants see your website. Beyond a classic SEO audit of crawling, indexing, content and links, it also tests whether AI crawlers can reach your pages, whether your answers are easy to quote, how AI tools describe your brand and which competitors they recommend instead. The result is a prioritised list of fixes for rankings and AI visibility together.

What an AI SEO audit checks

1. Search foundations

  • Indexing status of key pages and any noindex or canonical errors.
  • Core Web Vitals and mobile usability.
  • Title tags, headings and internal links.
  • Duplicate or competing pages.

2. Answer readiness (AEO)

  • Whether key pages open with a direct answer.
  • Question headings, lists and tables that engines can extract.
  • FAQ sections and structured data.
  • Featured snippets you hold and those competitors hold.

3. AI visibility (GEO)

  • How often AI tools mention your brand for buyer prompts.
  • Whether their description of you is accurate.
  • Which sources they cite, and where competitors appear instead.

4. AI access (AIO)

  • robots.txt rules for GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and Google-Extended.
  • Whether main content appears without JavaScript.
  • Organization, Person and Article schema.
  • Consistency of brand facts across the web.

What you get

A useful report gives you more than a score. Look for:

  1. A one-page summary of the biggest issues and opportunities.
  2. Fixes ranked by impact and effort.
  3. Examples from your own pages, not generic advice.
  4. A baseline of AI mentions you can track against later.
  5. A clear next step for each fix: who does it and how.

How long it takes

For a small business site of 20 to 50 pages, an AI SEO audit usually takes a few days. Large sites with many templates take longer, because each template needs its own checks. The prompt testing step also takes time, since each prompt should be run several times across tools.

How to act on the audit

Start with access and indexing problems, because nothing else works until pages can be crawled. Next, fix your most valuable pages: service pages, pricing and top guides, rewriting them answer-first. Then align brand facts across profiles. Finally, plan mention-building for the sources AI tools cite. Our technical SEO checklist and guide to answer-first content help with the first two steps.

How often to run one

Run a full audit once or twice a year, and a short check after any redesign or migration. In addition, track AI mentions monthly with a fixed prompt set, as described in our AI prompt set guide, so you notice changes between audits.
