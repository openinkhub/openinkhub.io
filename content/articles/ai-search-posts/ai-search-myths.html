AI search myths spread fast because the field is new and the tools change often. The most common ones claim that SEO is dead, that special files or tags guarantee AI citations, or that AI-written content is banned. Checked against what Google, OpenAI and other companies have actually said, most of these beliefs do not hold up.

Here are nine myths to drop, with what the evidence shows instead.

Myth 1: SEO is dead

AI tools still rely on crawled, indexed web pages. Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode use Google Search, and ChatGPT search and Perplexity run their own web retrieval. So strong SEO remains the base for AI visibility. Our comparison of GEO vs SEO explains the link.

Myth 2: You need special markup for AI Overviews

Google states that there are no extra requirements for AI features. A page needs to be indexed and eligible for a snippet, and normal best practices apply. See Google's guide to AI features and your website.

Myth 3: llms.txt guarantees AI citations

llms.txt is a proposal from 2024 for a file that points language models to key pages. No major search engine has confirmed that it uses the file to rank or cite content. It can be a harmless extra, but it is not a strategy. Our guide to llms.txt covers the detail.

Myth 4: Google bans AI-generated content

Google targets low-value content made at scale to manipulate rankings, however it was produced. Helpful content made with AI help is allowed. Our article on AI content quality explains the scaled content abuse policy.

Myth 5: Blocking GPTBot hides you from ChatGPT

OpenAI uses GPTBot for model training and OAI-SearchBot for ChatGPT search. Blocking GPTBot alone does not remove you from ChatGPT search results. Our guide to AI crawlers in robots.txt lists each bot.

Myth 6: FAQ schema still gets you rich results

Since August 2023, FAQ rich results show mainly for authoritative government and health sites. The content still helps, but the visual boost is gone for most businesses. Read does FAQ schema still work.

Myth 7: You can pay to appear in AI answers

Organic AI answers in ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity do not sell placement. Some platforms test ads in separate, labelled spots. Meanwhile, anyone promising guaranteed organic AI placement is selling something they cannot deliver.

Myth 8: Keyword stuffing helps AI find you

Language models read meaning, not keyword counts. Repeating phrases makes text harder to read and can break search spam rules. Instead, write clear answers that use natural language, as described in our guide to answer-first content.

Myth 9: AI visibility cannot be measured

It can, though not with classic rank trackers. A fixed set of prompts, run each month in each AI tool, gives a reliable trend. In addition, GA4 can show visits from AI tools. See our guides to the AI prompt set and AI traffic in GA4.

How to spot new AI search myths

  • Check whether the claim comes from the platform's own documentation.
  • Be wary of guarantees about rankings or citations.
  • Look for tests with a clear method, not single screenshots.
  • Ask whether the tactic would also help a human reader.
