Featured snippet optimization means shaping a page so Google picks one of its passages for the highlighted answer box. To do it, target question searches where you already rank on page one, answer the question in the right format directly under a matching heading, and keep that answer short, factual and complete. Paragraph, list and table snippets each need a slightly different approach.

How Google chooses a featured snippet

Google picks the snippet from pages it already ranks well, usually on the first page of results. It then looks for a passage that answers the query clearly on its own. So featured snippet optimization starts with rankings and finishes with formatting. Google explains the basics in its guide to featured snippets.

Because the snippet now often sits near an AI Overview, the same passage can serve both. Our guide to Google AI Overviews optimization covers that overlap.

Paragraph snippets

Paragraph snippets answer "what is", "why" and "how long" questions. To win one, place a 40 to 60 word answer right below a heading that repeats the question. Start with the subject, for example "Local SEO is...", and include the key fact in the first sentence. Our guide to answer-first content shows the pattern.

List snippets

List snippets answer "how to", "steps" and "best ways" questions. Use a real HTML ordered list for steps and an unordered list for options. Keep each item short, ideally under 12 words, and start each one with a verb or a noun. Then add detail in paragraphs below the list, not inside it.

Also, number your H3 headings for long processes, such as "Step 1: Pick your keywords". Google sometimes builds a list from those headings.

Table snippets

Table snippets answer comparison, price and specification questions. Use a proper HTML table with a header row and clear column names. Keep cells brief, and put the most important column first. For more on this format, see our guide to comparison content SEO.

Which pages to target first

  1. Open Search Console and filter for question queries.
  2. Keep pages ranking in positions 2 to 10 for those queries.
  3. Search each query and note the current snippet format.
  4. Rewrite your answer to match that format, but make it clearer.
  5. Recheck rankings and the snippet after two to four weeks.

This order works because you focus on pages Google already trusts. Our question research guide helps you build the query list.

Common mistakes that lose snippets

  • Burying the answer below a long introduction.
  • Using images of text, which Google cannot quote.
  • Building lists with line breaks instead of list tags.
  • Answering a different question from the one in the heading.
  • Letting facts go stale, so a fresher page takes over.

Can you lose traffic from a snippet?

Sometimes a snippet answers a question fully, so fewer people click. Even so, the snippet puts your brand at the top, and for complex topics it often raises clicks. If you truly want to limit it, the max-snippet robots rule controls how much text Google shows. Most sites, however, are better off competing for the box.

Track your featured snippet optimization

Keep a simple sheet of target queries, the page, the snippet owner and the date you checked. Review it each month.
