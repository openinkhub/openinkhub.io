AI crawlers in robots.txt: each major AI company runs one or more named bots, and you can allow or block each one with a User-agent rule in your robots.txt file. The key ones are GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot and ChatGPT-User from OpenAI, ClaudeBot from Anthropic, PerplexityBot from Perplexity, and the Google-Extended token from Google. Most businesses that want AI visibility allow the search bots and decide case by case on training bots.

What each AI crawler does

User agentCompanyPurpose
OAI-SearchBotOpenAIFinds pages for ChatGPT search results
GPTBotOpenAICollects content that may train future models
ChatGPT-UserOpenAIFetches a page when a user asks ChatGPT to open it
ClaudeBotAnthropicCollects web content for Anthropic's models
PerplexityBotPerplexityIndexes pages for Perplexity answers
Google-ExtendedGoogleControls use of content for Gemini models and grounding

Check each company's documentation before you write rules, since names and purposes change. OpenAI's list sits in its bots overview, and Google's in its common crawlers guide.

Search bots vs training bots

This split matters most. Search bots, such as OAI-SearchBot and PerplexityBot, fetch pages so an assistant can cite them in answers today. Training bots, such as GPTBot, gather content that may shape future models. So you can block training while still appearing in AI search results.

Google-Extended works differently. It is not a separate crawler; Googlebot does the fetching, and the token only tells Google whether it may use your content for Gemini. Blocking it does not change Google Search crawling or rankings.

A sample AI crawlers robots.txt policy

This example allows AI search and user-triggered fetches, but opts out of model training:

User-agent: OAI-SearchBot
Allow: /

User-agent: ChatGPT-User
Allow: /

User-agent: PerplexityBot
Allow: /

User-agent: GPTBot
Disallow: /

User-agent: Google-Extended
Disallow: /

User-agent: *
Allow: /
Sitemap: https://www.example.com/sitemap.xml

If you want maximum AI visibility, allow the training bots too. Many brands do, because models that learn about you may mention you more often. It is a business choice, not a technical one.

Common mistakes

  • Blocking every AI bot with a single rule, then wondering why assistants ignore you.
  • Assuming robots.txt stops scrapers that do not follow it.
  • Letting a firewall or CDN bot filter block AI search bots silently.
  • Forgetting staging or duplicate domains that need their own rules.

Does robots.txt stop every AI crawler?

No. robots.txt is a public request, not a lock. The major companies say their named bots follow it, but unknown scrapers may not. For those, use firewall or CDN rules that block by user agent and behaviour. Meanwhile, check your server logs to see which bots actually visit.

How to check your current rules

Review your AI crawlers robots.txt setup at least twice a year, because new bots appear often:

  1. Open yourdomain.com/robots.txt in a browser.
  2. Look for each AI user agent listed above.
  3. Check your CDN or security plugin for bot blocking settings.
  4. Review server logs for hits from these bots.
  5. Test a key page with a fetch using the bot's user agent string.
