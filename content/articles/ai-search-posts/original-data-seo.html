Original data SEO means publishing facts that only you have, such as survey results, anonymised platform data or careful price studies, so that journalists, bloggers and AI tools cite you as the source. It is one of the most reliable ways to earn links and brand mentions, because people need a source for every statistic they quote.

Why original research earns citations

Writers look for numbers to support their points. When they find a clear, recent statistic, they link to it and name the source. AI tools behave in a similar way: when asked "how much does X cost in India?", they favour pages with specific, dated figures. So a single strong study can earn mentions for years.

In addition, original data sets you apart from pages that repeat the same advice. That supports the trust signals in our EEAT audit checklist.

Types of studies small businesses can run

  • Customer surveys: ask 100 to 300 customers about habits, costs or pain points.
  • Platform data: share anonymised trends from your own service, such as average project timelines.
  • Price comparisons: collect public prices across providers in a clear, repeatable way.
  • Audits at scale: check 200 local websites for speed or mobile issues and report the results.
  • Expert panels: ask 20 practitioners the same five questions and summarise their answers.

The original data SEO playbook

  1. Pick a question people argue about. For example, "How long does local SEO take to show results?"
  2. Design a fair method. Decide your sample, time frame and definitions before you collect data.
  3. Collect and clean the data. Remove duplicates and incomplete answers.
  4. Find three to five headline findings. Each one should fit in a single sentence with a number.
  5. Publish a clear report page. Lead with the findings, then add charts, method and limits.
  6. Promote it. Send the findings to journalists, newsletter writers and creators in your niche.
  7. Update it yearly. A refreshed study keeps its links and earns new ones.

How to write AI-quotable findings

Write each finding as a complete sentence with the number, the group and the date. For instance, a finding written as "In our [year] survey of [number] small retailers in [city], [X]% said most new customers found them on Google Maps" works in a news story, a snippet and an AI answer. By contrast, a chart without text is hard for machines to quote.

Also, put the findings in HTML text and a simple table, not only in images. Our guide to answer-first content explains why this structure helps.

Show your method

Trust depends on transparency. So include the sample size, how you found respondents, the dates, the exact questions and any limits. A short method section also helps journalists decide whether to use your data. Meanwhile, avoid overclaiming; a survey of 200 people does not speak for a whole country.

Turn one study into many assets

One study can feed a report page, three blog posts, a LinkedIn carousel, a short video and a press note. Each asset links back to the main report. As a result, mentions spread across platforms that AI tools read, which supports the work in our guide to brand mentions in LLM answers.
